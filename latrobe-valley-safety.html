Safety alerts for The Latrobe Valley and Gippsland

Live incidents from the state’s official emergency data across Latrobe, Baw Baw, Wellington and South Gippsland, a warning before you enter a repeat-incident area, and your family’s position against whatever is happening.

What Spotter covers in the Latrobe Valley and Gippsland

The Latrobe Valley runs east from Warragul and Drouin through Moe, Morwell and Traralgon to Sale, about 150 km from Melbourne at its centre, with roughly 75,000 people in the main towns. It is Victoria’s power-generating region, built on brown coal, and the Strzelecki Ranges and the Great Dividing Range close in on either side.

Spotter reads the state’s official incident data — the same source behind its public warnings — and shows what is live near you across Latrobe, Baw Baw, Wellington and South Gippsland, including Traralgon, Morwell, Moe, Churchill, Newborough, Yallourn North, Glengarry, Rosedale, Sale, Maffra, Warragul, Drouin, Trafalgar, Yarragon and Boolarra.

The incidents that actually recur here

No other part of Victoria carries this combination. The open-cut mines and power stations make industrial incidents a routine category here rather than an occasional one. The forested ranges on both sides bring a serious bushfire profile — on Black Saturday in 2009 the Churchill fire became one of the deadliest of that day. And the valley’s shape traps smoke: in 2014 a fire in the Hazelwood open-cut mine at Morwell burned for 45 days, and the resulting air quality became a public health emergency that a state inquiry later found had been foreseeable.

Spotter orders all of it by distance rather than by when it was filed, and hides the operational-status and “0 ha” noise that makes the raw feed hard to read. When nothing is live nearby it says “Nothing reported near you” — never that you are safe.

Getting around Latrobe Valley

Traralgon, Morwell and Moe function as one dispersed town, with people moving between them daily and a long drive to Melbourne on the Princes Freeway or the Gippsland line. Mobile coverage is patchy once you climb into the Strzeleckis.

When the emergency lasts six weeks

The Hazelwood fire is the reason this page exists. It was not a single frightening night. It was a month and a half of bad air, with people making daily decisions about whether to keep children home, whether to leave, and whether today was worse than yesterday.

Spotter keeps the full alert history rather than only what is live right now, so you can look back at what has been running near a saved place over days and weeks instead of only what is on screen. Area Insights draws the same data as a heat map over time. Neither predicts anything — they show what was reported, which during a long emergency is the thing that is hardest to hold in your head.

Risk Radar

An area becomes a hotspot when three or more reports land inside roughly a one-kilometre cell within 180 days, and the warning circle is 1.2 km across. Spotter watches the 20 nearest of those using the phone’s own region monitoring, so warnings arrive with the app closed. There is no suburb score anywhere in Spotter: a single number across a whole postcode tells you nothing useful about the street you are on.

Spotter is not an emergency service. In an emergency, always call 000. Spotter does not replace your state’s official emergency app — it reads the same official data and puts it next to your family’s safety in one app, so you are not checking four sources at once.
