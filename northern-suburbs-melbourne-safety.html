Safety alerts for Melbourne's Northern Suburbs

Live incidents from Victoria’s official emergency feed across Hume, Whittlesea, Darebin, Moreland (Merri-bek), Banyule and Nillumbik, a warning before you enter a repeat-incident area, and your family’s position against whatever is happening.

What Spotter covers in Melbourne's Northern Suburbs

The north runs from Brunswick and Coburg, inner and dense, out through Preston, Reservoir and Thomastown to Epping, Mill Park and Mernda, with the Hume arm taking in Broadmeadows, Craigieburn, Roxburgh Park and Sunbury. The outer edge reaches genuine countryside around Whittlesea, Wallan and the Kinglake ranges.

Spotter reads the state’s official incident data — the same source behind its public warnings — and shows what is live near you across Hume, Whittlesea, Darebin, Moreland (Merri-bek), Banyule and Nillumbik, including Preston, Reservoir, Coburg, Brunswick, Thomastown, Epping, Mill Park, South Morang, Mernda, Craigieburn, Roxburgh Park, Broadmeadows, Sunbury, Whittlesea and Greenvale.

The incidents that actually recur here

The north has the sharpest urban-to-bushfire transition of any part of Melbourne. Suburbs like Mernda, Doreen, Whittlesea and Sunbury sit at or near the bushfire interface, and the Kinglake ranges beyond them carry the most serious fire history in the state. Closer in, around Broadmeadows and Campbellfield, industrial land again means structure fires and hazardous material incidents in the feed.

Spotter orders all of it by distance rather than by when it was filed, and hides the operational-status and “0 ha” noise that makes the raw feed hard to read. When nothing is live nearby it says “Nothing reported near you” — never that you are safe.

Getting around North

The Hume Freeway, the Craigieburn and Mernda lines and the Ring Road carry the north’s traffic, and plenty of households run between an outer estate and an inner workplace daily. Risk Radar fires as you enter a repeat-incident area with the app closed, using the phone’s own region monitoring, so it costs almost nothing in battery.

Living at the bushfire interface

If you are in Mernda, Doreen, Whittlesea, Sunbury or the Kinglake side, your summer is different from someone in Brunswick. The official warning levels — Advice, Watch and Act, Emergency Warning — are the source of record, and you should keep your state’s official emergency app for them. Spotter does not replace it.

What Spotter adds on those days is your people. If someone in your circle is within 1 km of a live incident, that becomes the top card with a one-tap “Are you OK?” message, and the reply reaches you without them having to find and type anything.

Risk Radar

An area becomes a hotspot when three or more reports land inside roughly a one-kilometre cell within 180 days, and the warning circle is 1.2 km across. Spotter watches the 20 nearest of those using the phone’s own region monitoring, so warnings arrive with the app closed. There is no suburb score anywhere in Spotter: a single number across a whole postcode tells you nothing useful about the street you are on.

Spotter is not an emergency service. In an emergency, always call 000. Spotter does not replace your state’s official emergency app — it reads the same official data and puts it next to your family’s safety in one app, so you are not checking four sources at once.
