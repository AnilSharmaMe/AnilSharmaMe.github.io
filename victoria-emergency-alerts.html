Victoria emergency alerts, on your phone

Spotter reads Victoria’s official incident feed and shows what is live near you and near your family — across Greater Melbourne and regional Victoria.

Where Victoria’s official alerts come from

Victoria publishes a live incident feed through VicEmergency, covering Fire Rescue Victoria, the CFA, VICSES and Ambulance Victoria responses. Spotter reads that feed directly and turns each incident into a card on your home screen.

The raw feed is noisier than most people expect. About 98% of entries carry an operational status — “Responding”, “Going”, “Under control” — and many carry a size field reading SMALL or 0 ha. Spotter hides those, because a card that says “0 ha” reads as if nothing happened. What is left is the warning level, the incident type, how far it is from you, and how fresh it is.

Should you use this instead of the VicEmergency app?

No — and we say so inside the app. VicEmergency is the official channel, it carries the formal Advice / Watch and Act / Emergency Warning levels, and during a bushfire it is the source of record. Keep it installed.

What Spotter adds is everything official feeds do not do:

  • Several sources in one place. VicEmergency incidents, the stolen vehicle register and public missing-persons lists, instead of three separate apps.
  • Your family’s position against the incident. An official alert tells you a fire is at a street address. Spotter tells you that your daughter is 450 m from it.
  • A warning before you drive in, via Risk Radar’s repeat-incident areas.
  • An “I’m OK” message on any live incident within 10 km, which tells your circle you are fine without sending them your location. The server writes the message from its own copy of the alert, so no text from your phone reaches your family.

Coverage across the state, and beyond it

Victoria has Spotter’s deepest coverage, because VicEmergency publishes the full range of incident types — structure fires, rescues, hazardous material incidents, flooding and grass fires. That applies across Greater Melbourne and regional Victoria: Geelong, Ballarat, Bendigo, the Latrobe Valley, Shepparton, Mildura and the towns between them.

Spotter also imports two other states’ official feeds, checked every five minutes:

  • New South Wales — major incidents from the NSW Rural Fire Service.
  • Queensland — bushfire alerts from the Queensland Fire Department.

Those two are fire-focused feeds, so outside Victoria you get major fire and bushfire warnings rather than the full incident mix. Each alert carries its source agency on the detail screen, as those feeds’ licences require. Community reports and all the family features work anywhere in Australia.

We would rather set that out precisely than claim uniform national coverage.

Bushfire season

Through a Victorian summer the feed gets busy, and distance is what makes it usable. Spotter shows the nearest live incident within 10 km on the home headline, and keeps the full history so you can see what has been running near a saved place over the past days rather than only what is on screen right now.

Area Insights draws the same data as a heat map, which is the honest way to look at a pattern over time — it shows where reports have clustered, without pretending to predict where the next one will be.

Spotter is not an emergency service. In an emergency, always call 000. Spotter does not replace VicEmergency, the CFA or Victoria Police — it reads the same official feeds and puts them next to your family’s safety in one app, so you are not checking four sources at once.
