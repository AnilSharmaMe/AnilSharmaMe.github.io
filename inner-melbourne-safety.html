Safety alerts for Inner Melbourne and the CBD

Live incidents from Victoria’s official emergency feed across City of Melbourne, Yarra, Port Phillip and Stonnington, a warning before you enter a repeat-incident area, and your family’s position against whatever is happening.

What Spotter covers in Inner Melbourne and the CBD

Inner Melbourne is the CBD grid plus the ring of dense suburbs around it — Carlton, Fitzroy, Collingwood, Richmond, South Yarra, Prahran, St Kilda, Southbank, Docklands and Port Melbourne. It has the city’s highest residential density, its highest proportion of apartment living, and a daytime population several times its resident one.

Spotter reads the state’s official incident data — the same source behind its public warnings — and shows what is live near you across City of Melbourne, Yarra, Port Phillip and Stonnington, including Melbourne CBD, Southbank, Docklands, Carlton, Fitzroy, Collingwood, Richmond, South Yarra, Prahran, St Kilda, Port Melbourne, North Melbourne, Parkville and Brunswick East.

The incidents that actually recur here

High-rise living changes which incidents matter. Structure fires in apartment towers, lift rescues and hazardous material incidents in basement car parks are the recurring types here, and an incident 200 m away in a dense grid affects far more people than the same incident in an outer suburb. Distance is also measured differently: 500 m in the CBD is a different thing from 500 m in Pakenham.

Spotter orders all of it by distance rather than by when it was filed, and hides the operational-status and “0 ha” noise that makes the raw feed hard to read. When nothing is live nearby it says “Nothing reported near you” — never that you are safe.

Getting around Inner city

Almost everyone in inner Melbourne moves on foot, by tram or by bike, often late at night, and a large share are students or recent arrivals without family nearby. The one-tap alert to your circle and the ability to share an approximate suburb-level position rather than an exact one were both built with that in mind.

If you are new to the city

International students and new arrivals get the worst version of this problem: no local network, unfamiliar geography, and advice that is either scare stories or nothing at all. Spotter will not hand you a ranked list of dangerous streets, because that list would be mostly wrong.

What it does is show what is actually live near you right now, and let you connect a circle — including people overseas — who get told if you are near an incident. Every connection needs mutual approval, you can pause sharing at any time, and removing someone takes effect immediately without telling them.

Risk Radar

An area becomes a hotspot when three or more reports land inside roughly a one-kilometre cell within 180 days, and the warning circle is 1.2 km across. Spotter watches the 20 nearest of those using the phone’s own region monitoring, so warnings arrive with the app closed. There is no suburb score anywhere in Spotter: a single number across a whole postcode tells you nothing useful about the street you are on.

Spotter is not an emergency service. In an emergency, always call 000. Spotter does not replace your state’s official emergency app — it reads the same official data and puts it next to your family’s safety in one app, so you are not checking four sources at once.
