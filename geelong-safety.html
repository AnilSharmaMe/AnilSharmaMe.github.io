Safety alerts for Geelong and the Bellarine

Live incidents from the state’s official emergency data across Greater Geelong, the Surf Coast and the Borough of Queenscliffe, a warning before you enter a repeat-incident area, and your family’s position against whatever is happening.

What Spotter covers in Geelong and the Bellarine

Geelong is Victoria’s second city, about 75 km south west of Melbourne, with roughly 290,000 people across Greater Geelong. It runs from the industrial northern suburbs at Corio and Norlane, through the central city on Corio Bay, out to the Bellarine Peninsula at Ocean Grove, Drysdale and Queenscliff, and down the Surf Coast to Torquay and Anglesea.

Spotter reads the state’s official incident data — the same source behind its public warnings — and shows what is live near you across Greater Geelong, the Surf Coast and the Borough of Queenscliffe, including Geelong, Corio, Norlane, Belmont, Newtown, Grovedale, Waurn Ponds, Lara, Ocean Grove, Barwon Heads, Drysdale, Portarlington, Queenscliff, Torquay and Anglesea.

The incidents that actually recur here

Three different risks sit within half an hour of each other here, which is unusual. The northern suburbs hold heavy industry — the Corio refinery and the port — so hazardous material incidents and large structure fires appear in the feed more often than the residential population would suggest. The Surf Coast behind Anglesea and the eastern Otways is heathland and forest, with a serious summer bushfire profile and limited roads out. And the whole coast from Barwon Heads round to Queenscliff brings water rescues and coastal warnings through summer.

Spotter orders all of it by distance rather than by when it was filed, and hides the operational-status and “0 ha” noise that makes the raw feed hard to read. When nothing is live nearby it says “Nothing reported near you” — never that you are safe.

Getting around Geelong

A large number of people commute to Melbourne daily on the Geelong line or the Princes Freeway, and in summer the population of the Bellarine and Surf Coast towns multiplies. Saved places for Home, School and Work give you an arrival notice without anyone having to remember to message, which matters more when the household is split across a 75 km commute.

A holiday town in January is a different place

Torquay, Anglesea, Ocean Grove and Barwon Heads swell enormously over summer. That is exactly when visitors have the least local knowledge, when families get separated on crowded beaches, and when the bushfire risk behind the towns is at its highest.

Spotter shows the nearest live incident within 10 km on the home screen, and Risk Radar warns you as you enter a repeat-incident area even with the app closed. If you are down for a week rather than living here, that is local knowledge you do not otherwise have.

Risk Radar

An area becomes a hotspot when three or more reports land inside roughly a one-kilometre cell within 180 days, and the warning circle is 1.2 km across. Spotter watches the 20 nearest of those using the phone’s own region monitoring, so warnings arrive with the app closed. There is no suburb score anywhere in Spotter: a single number across a whole postcode tells you nothing useful about the street you are on.

Spotter is not an emergency service. In an emergency, always call 000. Spotter does not replace your state’s official emergency app — it reads the same official data and puts it next to your family’s safety in one app, so you are not checking four sources at once.
