Safety alerts for Melbourne's Eastern Suburbs

Live incidents from Victoria’s official emergency feed across Whitehorse, Manningham, Maroondah, Knox, Monash and Yarra Ranges, a warning before you enter a repeat-incident area, and your family’s position against whatever is happening.

What Spotter covers in Melbourne's Eastern Suburbs

The east runs from Box Hill and Doncaster through Ringwood and Croydon to Lilydale, then climbs into the Dandenong Ranges at Belgrave, Olinda and Sassafras and continues to Healesville and the Yarra Valley. The character changes completely over that distance — from one of Melbourne’s densest activity centres to forested hills.

Spotter reads Victoria’s official incident feed — the same VicEmergency data behind the state’s warnings — and shows what is live near you across Whitehorse, Manningham, Maroondah, Knox, Monash and Yarra Ranges, including Box Hill, Doncaster, Blackburn, Nunawading, Glen Waverley, Wantirna, Ringwood, Croydon, Bayswater, Boronia, Lilydale, Mooroolbark, Belgrave, Olinda and Healesville.

The incidents that actually recur here

The Dandenong Ranges are the defining risk. Heavily treed, steep, with narrow roads and a single way out of many streets, they produce two recurring emergency types: bushfire through summer, and storm damage with trees down and extended power outages through winter. The June 2021 storms cut power to parts of the ranges for well over a week. Further down, around Bayswater and Boronia, industrial land brings the usual structure fire and hazardous material incidents.

Spotter orders all of it by distance rather than by when it was filed, and hides the operational-status and “0 ha” noise that makes the raw feed hard to read. When nothing is live nearby it says “Nothing reported near you” — never that you are safe.

Getting around East

The Belgrave and Lilydale lines and the Eastern Freeway and EastLink carry the east, and many ranges households drive down the hill daily. The practical value of arrival notices is higher here than almost anywhere — mobile coverage in the hills is patchy, and “got home fine” is a message that often does not send.

What a week without power actually needs

Ranges residents already know the drill: trees down, roads cut, power out for days. The gap in that situation is not information about the storm, it is knowing where everyone is and whether they got out.

Spotter keeps the full alert history rather than only what is live, so you can see what has been running near a saved place over several days. And an “I’m OK” message to your circle sends no location at all — the server writes the text from its own copy of the alert — which matters when you are trying to send something small over a bad connection.

Risk Radar

An area becomes a hotspot when three or more reports land inside roughly a one-kilometre cell within 180 days, and the warning circle is 1.2 km across. Spotter watches the 20 nearest of those using the phone’s own region monitoring, so warnings arrive with the app closed. There is no suburb score anywhere in Spotter: a single number across a whole postcode tells you nothing useful about the street you are on.

Spotter is not an emergency service. In an emergency, always call 000. Spotter does not replace VicEmergency, the CFA or Victoria Police — it reads the same official feeds and puts them next to your family’s safety in one app, so you are not checking four sources at once.
